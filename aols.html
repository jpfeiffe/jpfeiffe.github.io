🚨 What Changed — Read This First

Arrow of Light is no longer part of Webelos. If you ran a Webelos den last year, these are the differences that will catch you out.

⚠️ This is the year your Scout finds a troop

That is the headline. Everything else on this page is detail by comparison.

Arrow of Light is built as a bridge year. The rank exists to get your Scout ready to join a Scouts BSA troop between January and March — not next year, this year. Visiting a troop is a rank requirement (Bobcat 7), and Scouting America wants that visit in the early fall, right at the start of the program year.

So the work starts now: find troops, visit them, and pick one. Families should expect to shop around — troops differ enormously in size, how much the youth run things, and how often they camp. Visiting more than the one required is a good idea, not an imposition. By roughly February your Scout should know where they are going.

👖 Uniform — optional, not a requirement

Scouting America describes Arrow of Light Scouts as wearing the Scouts BSA uniform with blue shoulder loops. Our pack does not require it — blues are traditional here and remain perfectly fine all year.

Worth considering, though: a Scouts BSA uniform bought now carries over to their troop and patrol after crossover, so it is not money spent on a single year. If a family is going to buy one anyway, buying it now and sizing for growth is the sensible time.

⏳ The year is shorter than you think

The rank is designed to prepare Scouts to join Scouts BSA between January and March. That is six required adventures in roughly five months — not a full September-to-May run.

👥 A patrol, not a den

5th graders are Arrow of Light Scouts and are introduced to the patrol method — patrol name, elected patrol leader, patrol code of conduct. Bobcat sets this up and must be done first.

📖 Separate handbook

Arrow of Light has its own handbook. The Webelos book no longer covers it, and Webelos and Arrow of Light no longer share elective adventures.

🏕 Camping rules still Cub Scout rules

At least one adult on a pack or Arrow of Light den campout must be BALOO-trained. And when camping with a Scouts BSA troop, Arrow of Light Scouts still follow Arrow of Light Cub Scout camping rules — troop rules do not apply to them.

🤝 Working With a Scouts BSA Troop

Troop partnership runs through this rank far more than it did through Webelos. It is worth being precise about what is required versus what is recommended, because only one of those has to be on the calendar.

Required: the troop visit

Bobcat requirement 7 — with your patrol, or with a parent or guardian, visit a Scouts BSA troop.

Scouting America is explicit about when: the troop visit belongs in the early fall, at the beginning of the program year. Bobcat is also the adventure the patrol must complete first.

Three ways to satisfy it:

  • Attend a troop daytime activity
  • Do a troop meeting scavenger hunt during a visit
  • Attend a troop overnight

✅ Yes — make the troop visit your campout prep night

Most of the troop-involving items are Outdoor Adventurer prep, and they fold into the same evening as the required Bobcat troop visit. One night at a troop meeting can carry:

  • Bobcat 7 — visit a Scouts BSA troop (required — this is the whole requirement)
  • Outdoor Adventurer 1 — Scout Basic Essentials, taught by a Scout
  • Outdoor Adventurer 2 — the gear shakedown: what to bring, how to carry it
  • Outdoor Adventurer 3 — review the four points of the SAFE Checklist
  • Outdoor Adventurer 4 — find the campsite on a map

That is a required requirement plus half of Outdoor Adventurer in one visit, taught by Scouts who camp every month — which is exactly the experience the rank is trying to give them.

What will not fit in that night:

  • Outdoor Adventurer 5–8 — the campout itself
  • Citizenship 2 — two hours of service; a separate outing, though it can be done alongside a troop
  • First Aid 2–7 — needs its own session. A troop adult with Wilderness or Standard First Aid certification is BSA’s suggested instructor, and the campout is a natural place for it

No requirement obliges Scouts BSA youth to help, and there is no den chief requirement — Bobcat 7 accepts a parent and Outdoor Adventurer 5 accepts the patrol. But a partner troop collapses a lot of calendar, and solves the First Aid instructor problem at the same time.

📚 What Goes Home

Two requirements carry explicit “at home” wording, one whole adventure — Duty to God — is the family-delivered one, and two more quietly land at home without being labelled that way:

Bobcat, requirement 8

With a parent or guardian, work through the exercises in A Parent’s Guide.

First Aid, requirement 1

With your parent or guardian’s permission, watch the Protect Yourself Rules video for Arrow of Light.

Duty to God — the whole adventure

Scouting America: “This Adventure is commonly done at home with the Cub Scout’s family. If it is being done as a den, ensure that every parent and guardian is aware of the content and the activities that the den will do and allow parents to opt out of doing it as a den activity and choose to complete the requirement at home.”

This is the Family & Reverence Adventure — the same slot My Family filled in Webelos, which is why it sat in the at-home bucket last year. Official requirements →

The three requirements:

  1. Discuss your family’s faith traditions with a parent or guardian. Pick a value of that tradition tied to the Scout Law and discuss how each family member demonstrates it.
  2. Meet a representative of a local faith-based organization that helps people in crisis regardless of their faith. Identify who they help and how.
  3. Discuss with a parent, guardian, or adult leader what Duty to God means to you, and how you practice it daily.
Religious emblem substitution — check before relying on it. The Webelos My Family page states the adventure may be earned “by completing the requirements below OR by completing a Religious Emblem.” The Arrow of Light Duty to God page carries no such clause. Older guidance describing an emblem substitute refers to the retired Duty to God in Action adventure. Confirm with the handbook or your council before signing anyone off on an emblem alone.

Personal Fitness 2 and 4 — in practice

Requirement 2 is a 14-day activity log kept at home between meetings, then shared back with the patrol or family. Requirement 4 is reviewing the Annual Health and Medical Record with a parent.

Neither carries BSA’s formal “at home” wording, so they are easy to overlook — but both land with families. Hand out the tracking sheet at least two weeks before you want Personal Fitness signed off.

⚠ Two planning notes:
  • First Aid is not a parent-homework adventure. Only requirement 1 is at home — the hurry cases, choking, shock, treating cuts and burns, and the personal first aid kit are all hands-on demonstrations that need den meeting time.
  • Duty to God cannot go home entirely. Requirements 1 and 3 are family discussions, but requirement 2 — meeting a representative of a local faith-based organization — has to be arranged. If the den hands the whole adventure to families, that visit is the piece that quietly does not happen.

🏕 Required Adventures

Requirements below are paraphrased — follow the official text when signing off. Duty to God is covered in What Goes Home above, and Outdoor Adventurer has its own section below — together with these four, that is all six.

AT HOME done with a parent or guardian    14 DAYS AT HOME needs a two-week head start    AT CAMP best completed on the campout    TROOP NIGHT doable at the troop visit    STACKS can be run alongside another adventure

Bobcat

  1. Form a patrol: pick a name, elect a patrol leader, and discuss why the patrol method works.
  2. Get to know the members of your patrol.
  3. Recite the Scout Oath and Scout Law with your patrol.
  4. Build a patrol code of conduct.
  5. Demonstrate the Scouts BSA sign, salute, and handshake, and explain when each is used.
  6. Learn the Scouts BSA motto and slogan.
  7. Visit a Scouts BSA troop — with your patrol or with a parent or guardian.
  8. AT HOMEWork through A Parent’s Guide with a parent or guardian.
Do this adventure first. Bobcat sets up the patrol; the other five may be done in any order afterward. Requirement 7, the troop visit, belongs in the early fall at the start of the program year — it may be satisfied by a troop daytime activity, a troop meeting scavenger hunt, or a troop overnight.

Citizenship

  1. Pick a service project your patrol or pack could do, and plan it using the BSA SAFE Checklist.
  2. Serve at least two hours total. Time may be split across more than one project.
📅 Dated option: 10,000 for the Troops is a national service option for requirement 2 — holiday cards for military personnel. Letters must be mailed to the Scouting America National Service Center by November 6, 2026. A service project alongside a Scouts BSA troop is another listed option.

First Aid

  1. AT HOMEWith parent or guardian permission, watch the Protect Yourself Rules video for Arrow of Light.
  2. Explain what to do when you find someone who needs first aid.
  3. Demonstrate the hurry cases: serious bleeding, heart attack or cardiac arrest, stopped breathing, stroke, and poisoning.
  4. Demonstrate how to help a choking victim.
  5. Demonstrate how to treat shock.
  6. Demonstrate how to treat cuts and scratches, burns and scalds, bites and stings, and a nosebleed.
  7. Build a personal first aid kit and show the use of each item.
Who teaches this: Scouting America suggests bringing in a Scouts BSA troop adult leader holding Wilderness or Standard First Aid certification, a medical professional, an EMT, or another adult with formal first aid training. Share the adventure content with them so it stays age-appropriate.

Personal Fitness

  1. AT CAMPPlan a balanced meal you would eat when camping, and prepare it using the gear you would use on a campout.
  2. 14 DAYS AT HOMEExamine what being physically fit means and how you build it into your life. Track how many times you are active for 30 minutes or longer over a 14-day period. Share with your patrol or family what you enjoyed and whether you feel you are living up to the Scout Oath of being physically strong.
  3. Be active for 30 minutes with your patrol, a younger den, or one other person — include stretching and moving.
  4. AT HOMEReview your Annual Health and Medical Record with a parent or guardian and discuss your ability to take part in patrol and pack activities.
⏰ Start requirement 2 at least two weeks early. The 14-day tracking window runs at home between meetings, so it has to be handed out well before you want the adventure finished — and the share-back with the patrol or family needs a slot on the calendar afterward. This is the requirement most likely to be the last thing outstanding.

Requirement 4, the Annual Health and Medical Record review, is also a parent conversation at home. Neither carries BSA’s formal “at home” wording, but in practice both land with families.
🔁 Knock out requirement 1 at the Outdoor Adventurer campout. The patrol is already cooking with camp gear there, and Outdoor Adventurer 7 has them demonstrating food safety and a sanitary kitchen at the same time. Plan the menu as part of the campout prep and both requirements are covered in one trip.

Not strictly required to be at a campout — the requirement says a meal you would eat camping, prepared with the gear you would use, and Scouting America lists preparing a meal for a picnic as an alternative activity. So this is an opportunity to stack, not an obligation.
THE BIG ONE

⛰ Outdoor Adventurer

This is the anchor of the year. It is the only required adventure that cannot be done in a meeting room, it takes the most planning, and it is the one other requirements can be hung off. Plan the campout first and build the calendar around it.

The eight requirements official text →

  1. TROOP NIGHTLearn the Scout Basic Essentials.
  2. TROOP NIGHTDecide what to bring on an overnight — tent, sleeping gear — and how to carry it.
  3. TROOP NIGHTReview the four points of the SAFE Checklist and how they apply on the campout.
  4. TROOP NIGHTFind the campsite on a map.
  5. Camp with your patrol or with a Scouts BSA troop.
  6. On arrival, lay out the site — kitchen, eating area, tents, firepit — and set up patrol gear before your own tent.
  7. STACKSExplain food safety and a sanitary kitchen, and demonstrate it at camp.
  8. Afterward, debrief with your patrol: what worked, what you would change, and how you followed the Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace.

🔗 What you can tie into this one campout

Everything below is a requirement from another adventure that the campout can carry. Run them together and the trip does the work of several meetings.

Personal Fitness 1
Plan a balanced camp meal and cook it with camp gear. Same kitchen as requirement 7 — one activity, two requirements.
First Aid 2–7
The hands-on skills. A troop adult with Wilderness or Standard First Aid certification is BSA’s suggested instructor, and camp is the natural setting.
Citizenship 2
Two hours of service. A conservation project at the campsite can count — plan it with the SAFE Checklist as requirement 1 asks.
Personal Fitness 3
Thirty minutes active with the patrol, including stretching and moving. A hike from camp covers it.
⚠ Requirement 5 needs a patrol. The campout must be with the patrol or a Scouts BSA troop. Families camping on the same site does not satisfy it unless the Arrow of Light Scouts camp as a patrol. Worth settling before booking anything.
Camping rules. At least one adult must be BALOO-trained. And Arrow of Light Scouts follow Arrow of Light Cub Scout camping rules even when camping with a troop — troop rules do not apply to them.
🏕 Requirements 1–4 are prep. All four can be done at the troop visit night described above, alongside the required Bobcat 7. By the time you reach the campsite, half this adventure is already signed off.
