🚨 What Changed — Read This First

Arrow of Light is no longer part of Webelos. If you ran a Webelos den last year, these are the differences that will catch you out.

👖 New uniform

Arrow of Light Scouts wear the Scouts BSA uniform with blue shoulder loops on the epaulets — not the blue Cub Scout uniform. Budget and plan for this early; it is a real cost for families.

⏳ The year is shorter than you think

The rank is designed to prepare Scouts to join Scouts BSA between January and March. That is six required adventures in roughly five months — not a full September-to-May run.

👥 A patrol, not a den

5th graders are Arrow of Light Scouts and are introduced to the patrol method — patrol name, elected patrol leader, patrol code of conduct. Bobcat sets this up and must be done first.

📖 Separate handbook

Arrow of Light has its own handbook. The Webelos book no longer covers it, and Webelos and Arrow of Light no longer share elective adventures.

🏕 Camping rules still Cub Scout rules

At least one adult on a pack or Arrow of Light den campout must be BALOO-trained. And when camping with a Scouts BSA troop, Arrow of Light Scouts still follow Arrow of Light Cub Scout camping rules — troop rules do not apply to them.

🚫 No starting early

4th grade Webelos may not begin Arrow of Light until they have completed 4th grade.

🤝 Working With a Scouts BSA Troop

Troop partnership runs through this rank far more than it did through Webelos. It is worth being precise about what is required versus what is recommended, because only one of those has to be on the calendar.

Required: the troop visit

Bobcat requirement 7 — with your patrol, or with a parent or guardian, visit a Scouts BSA troop.

Scouting America is explicit about when: the troop visit belongs in the early fall, at the beginning of the program year. Bobcat is also the adventure the patrol must complete first.

Three ways to satisfy it:

  • Attend a troop daytime activity
  • Do a troop meeting scavenger hunt during a visit
  • Attend a troop overnight

Recommended: Scouts helping deliver adventures

No requirement obliges Scouts BSA youth to help, and there is no den chief requirement. But troop involvement appears throughout the suggested activity options:

  • Outdoor Adventurer 1 — learn the Scout Basic Essentials from a member of a Scout troop
  • Outdoor Adventurer — attend a troop meeting for a camping gear shakedown
  • Outdoor Adventurer 5 — camp with a Scouts BSA troop
  • Citizenship 2 — serve on a project alongside a Scouts BSA troop
  • First Aid — bring in a troop adult leader with Wilderness or Standard First Aid certification to teach the skills

You are not blocked without a troop partner. Both troop-referencing requirements offer an alternative — Bobcat 7 accepts a parent, Outdoor Adventurer 5 accepts the patrol. But the program is built assuming a partner troop, and finding one solves the First Aid teaching problem at the same time.

📚 The Two At-Home Requirements

Unlike Webelos, Arrow of Light hands no whole adventure to the family. Exactly two requirements are designated to be done at home with a parent or guardian:

Bobcat, requirement 8

With a parent or guardian, work through the exercises in A Parent’s Guide.

First Aid, requirement 1

With your parent or guardian’s permission, watch the Protect Yourself Rules video for Arrow of Light.

⚠ Planning note: First Aid is not a parent-homework adventure. Only requirement 1 is at home — the hurry cases, choking, shock, treating cuts and burns, and the personal first aid kit are all hands-on demonstrations that need den meeting time. Duty to God is likewise a den-supportable adventure built on family discussion, not an adventure handed to the family outright.

🏕 Required Adventures (all six)

Every Arrow of Light Scout completes all six. Requirements below are paraphrased — follow the official text when signing off.

Bobcat

  1. Form a patrol: pick a name, elect a patrol leader, and discuss why the patrol method works.
  2. Get to know the members of your patrol.
  3. Recite the Scout Oath and Scout Law with your patrol.
  4. Build a patrol code of conduct.
  5. Demonstrate the Scouts BSA sign, salute, and handshake, and explain when each is used.
  6. Learn the Scouts BSA motto and slogan.
  7. Visit a Scouts BSA troop — with your patrol or with a parent or guardian.
  8. AT HOMEWork through A Parent’s Guide with a parent or guardian.
Do this adventure first. Bobcat sets up the patrol; the other five may be done in any order afterward. Requirement 7, the troop visit, belongs in the early fall at the start of the program year — it may be satisfied by a troop daytime activity, a troop meeting scavenger hunt, or a troop overnight.

Citizenship

  1. Pick a service project your patrol or pack could do, and plan it using the BSA SAFE Checklist.
  2. Serve at least two hours total. Time may be split across more than one project.
📅 Dated option: 10,000 for the Troops is a national service option for requirement 2 — holiday cards for military personnel. Letters must be mailed to the Scouting America National Service Center by November 6, 2026. A service project alongside a Scouts BSA troop is another listed option.

Duty to God

Or substitute an appropriate religious emblem.

  1. Discuss your family’s faith traditions with a parent or guardian. Pick a value tied to the Scout Law and discuss how your family lives it.
  2. Meet someone from a local faith-based organization that serves people in crisis regardless of their faith. Learn who they help and how.
  3. Discuss what Duty to God means to you and how you practice it daily.

First Aid

  1. AT HOMEWith parent or guardian permission, watch the Protect Yourself Rules video for Arrow of Light.
  2. Explain what to do when you find someone who needs first aid.
  3. Demonstrate the hurry cases: serious bleeding, heart attack or cardiac arrest, stopped breathing, stroke, and poisoning.
  4. Demonstrate how to help a choking victim.
  5. Demonstrate how to treat shock.
  6. Demonstrate how to treat cuts and scratches, burns and scalds, bites and stings, and a nosebleed.
  7. Build a personal first aid kit and show the use of each item.
Who teaches this: Scouting America suggests bringing in a Scouts BSA troop adult leader holding Wilderness or Standard First Aid certification, a medical professional, an EMT, or another adult with formal first aid training. Share the adventure content with them so it stays age-appropriate.

Outdoor Adventurer

  1. Learn the Scout Basic Essentials.
  2. Decide what to bring on an overnight — tent, sleeping gear — and how to carry it.
  3. Review the four points of the SAFE Checklist and how they apply on the campout.
  4. Find the campsite on a map.
  5. Camp with your patrol or with a Scouts BSA troop.
  6. On arrival, lay out the site — kitchen, eating area, tents, firepit — and set up patrol gear before your own tent.
  7. Explain food safety and a sanitary kitchen, and demonstrate it at camp.
  8. Afterward, debrief with your patrol: what worked, what you would change, and how you followed the Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace.
Note on requirement 5: the campout must be with the patrol or a Scouts BSA troop. Families camping on the same site does not satisfy it unless the Arrow of Light Scouts camp as a patrol.
Camping rules and troop help: at least one adult must be BALOO-trained, and Arrow of Light Scouts follow Arrow of Light Cub Scout camping rules even when camping with a troop. Suggested troop-supported options include learning the Basic Essentials from a Scout and attending a troop meeting for a gear shakedown.

Personal Fitness

  1. Plan a balanced camping meal and cook it using camp gear.
  2. Track activity sessions of 30 minutes or more over 14 days. Share with your patrol or family whether you are living up to “physically fit.”
  3. Be active for 30 minutes with your patrol, a younger den, or one other person — include stretching and moving.
  4. Review the BSA Annual Health and Medical Record with a parent or guardian and discuss your participation.
