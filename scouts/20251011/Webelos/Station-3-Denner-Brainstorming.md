# Station 3: Denner Brainstorming

**Requirement:** Requirement 5 - Learn About the Denner Role  
**Time:** 12 minutes per rotation (4 rotations total)  
**Group Size:** 4-5 scouts per rotation  
**Location:** Table or quiet corner

---

## Your Role

You'll teach scouts about the important role of a "denner" (den helper/leader) and have them brainstorm responsibilities. This is a discussion and writing activity that prepares them for a full-group vote later.

---

## Materials Provided

**For Each Scout:**
- [ ] 1 half-sheet of paper
- [ ] 1 pencil or pen
- [ ] 1 clipboard (for writing surface)

**For Collection:**
- [ ] Ziplock bag or folder to collect finished papers

---

## Activity Instructions

### Before Scouts Arrive (1 minute)
1. Have all materials ready on the table
2. Keep papers protected until scouts arrive
3. Have clipboards available

### When Scouts Arrive at Your Station (1 minute)
1. Welcome: "Hi everyone! You're at Station 3: Denner Ideas!"
2. Quick intro: "We're going to learn about an important role called the denner."

### Part 1: Explain the Denner (4 minutes)

**Start with a Question:**
"Has anyone here ever been a class helper or line leader at school?"
- Let scouts share briefly (30 seconds)

**Explain What a Denner Is:**
"A denner is a Cub Scout who helps the den leader during meetings. It's a special job that everyone will get to do at least once!"

**Key Points to Share:**
1. ✅ **It's a helper role** - The denner assists the leader, but is NOT in charge of other scouts
2. ✅ **Everyone gets a turn** - Each scout will be denner for at least one meeting
3. ✅ **It's an honor** - Being denner means setting a good example
4. ✅ **Leadership by example** - The denner shows others how to follow the Scout Oath and Law
5. ✅ **Special cord** - The denner wears a yellow cord over their left shoulder

**Examples of Denner Jobs:**
Share these examples to get scouts thinking:
- Arrive early to help set up
- Welcome everyone when they arrive
- Lead the den in the Scout Oath and Scout Law
- Carry the United States flag during opening
- Pick a game for the den to play
- Help hand out supplies for activities
- Stay after the meeting to help clean up
- Set a good example by being friendly to everyone

**Ask:** "Why do you think these jobs are important?"
- Let scouts share ideas (1-2 minutes)

### Part 2: Brainstorm Ideas (6 minutes)

**Give Each Scout:**
- 1 half-sheet of paper
- 1 pencil
- 1 clipboard

**The Task:**
"Now it's YOUR turn to think! Write down THREE jobs that you think a denner should do."

**Instructions:**
1. "Think about what would be helpful during a den meeting"
2. "Write down 3 jobs - one idea per line"
3. "Write your name on the paper so we know it's yours"

**Your Role While They Write:**
- Walk around and help scouts who are stuck
- Offer suggestions if needed (from the examples above)
- Help with spelling if asked
- Encourage creative thinking: "What would make meetings more fun?"

**Prompts for Scouts Who Are Stuck:**
- "What do you wish someone would help with at meetings?"
- "Think about the beginning, middle, and end of a meeting"
- "What would make everyone feel welcome?"

### Part 3: Share Ideas (4-5 minutes)

**Once Most Scouts Have Finished:**
"Let's hear some of your ideas!"

**Go Around the Group:**
- Each scout shares ONE idea from their paper
- You can say: "Great idea!" or "That would be really helpful!"
- Keep it moving - don't let one scout dominate

**Discussion Question (if time):**
"What makes a good leader?"
- Listen to responses
- Connect to Scout Law: "A good leader is trustworthy, helpful, friendly..."

### Wrap-Up (1 minute)
1. Collect all papers in the ziplock bag
2. "Great job! Later, the whole den will vote on the top 5 jobs"
3. Make sure all 6 papers are collected

## Quick Reference: What is a Denner?

**Definition:** A Cub Scout who helps the den leader during a den meeting

**Key Facts:**
- Every scout gets a turn
- Chosen by den leader (could be rotation, volunteer, or other method)
- Wears a yellow denner cord over left shoulder
- Helps with specific jobs decided by the den leader
- Sets a good example for others
- Is NOT in charge of other scouts

**The Most Important Part:**
The denner's greatest responsibility is to set an example by living the Scout Oath and Scout Law - this is called "leadership by example"

---

## Tips for Success

✅ **Keep it Positive:** This should feel like an honor, not a chore!

✅ **Everyone Participates:** Make sure every scout writes something

✅ **No Wrong Answers:** All ideas are valuable for the later discussion

✅ **Help with Writing:** Some scouts may struggle - offer to help spell or even write for them

✅ **Make it Personal:** "When you're denner, what would YOU want to do?"

✅ **Connect to Real Life:** "This is like being a helper in class or at home"

---

## Common Questions & Answers

**Q: "Do I have to be a denner?"**  
A: Yes, everyone gets a turn! But don't worry - you'll get to help decide what the denner does, and we'll help you.

**Q: "How long do you have to be denner?"**  
A: Usually just one meeting, but your den leader decides. Everyone gets a turn.

**Q: "What if I don't want to do a certain job?"**  
A: That's okay! Later we'll vote on the jobs, and the denner only does the ones we all agree on.

**Q: "Can two scouts be denner together?"**  
A: Sometimes dens have a denner and an assistant denner! Your den leader will decide.

**Q: "What's the yellow cord?"**  
A: It's a special shoulder cord that shows who the denner is. It gets passed from one denner to the next.

**Q: "I can't think of any ideas!"**  
A: That's okay! Let me help you. Think about: What needs to be done at the start of a meeting? During activities? At the end?

## Rotation Transition

**When Time is Called:**
1. Collect ALL papers (count them - should have 6)
2. Put papers in the ziplock bag
3. Write the rotation number on the bag if you have multiple groups
4. Give the collected papers to the Den Leader
5. Reset materials for next group

---

## Important Notes

⚠️ **Collect ALL Papers:** The Den Leader needs these for the full-group activity later

⚠️ **Names on Papers:** Make sure scouts write their names

⚠️ **Keep Papers Safe:** Don't lose them - they're needed later!

## Closing Note
Collect papers every time; accuracy of ideas matters less than participation. Encourage brief sharing—keep rotation on schedule.
