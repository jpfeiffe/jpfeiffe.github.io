# Station 2: Teach the Cub Scout Sign, Salute, and Handshake

**Requirement:** Requirement 6 - Teach and demonstrate Cub Scout Sign, Salute, and Handshake  
**Time:** 12 minutes per rotation (4 rotations total)  
**Group Size:** 4-5 Webelos (pairing concept; younger Cub partners simulated if not present)  
**Location:** Open space or table area

---

## Your Role

You will coach Webelos Scouts to TEACH these three Cub Scout fundamentals as if they are helping a younger Cub (Lion/Tiger/Wolf/Bear). Emphasis is on clear demonstration, patience, and leadership by example—not just doing the actions themselves.

---

## Materials

- [ ] Printed "Cub Scout Sign, Salute, and Handshake" worksheet (one per Webelos) OR handbook reference
- [ ] Reference sheet (`Cub-Scout-Fundamentals-Reference.md`)
- [ ] Pens (optional for making teaching notes)

If actual younger Cubs are present: ensure one younger Cub per Webelos. If not, have Webelos pair and alternate roles (Teacher / Younger Cub).

---

## Activity Flow (12 Minutes)

### 0:00–1:00 Welcome & Framing
Say: "At this station you're going to practice being leaders by TEACHING the Cub Scout sign, salute, and handshake to a younger Cub Scout. Leadership is about patience, clarity, and encouragement."

Explain roles:
- If younger Cubs present: Pair each Webelos with one younger Cub.
- If not: Form pairs; one Webelos is "Instructor" and one pretends to be a younger Cub. Switch halfway.

### 1:00–3:00 Demonstration by You
Model each fundamental slowly while scouts mimic:
1. Sign – Explain purpose (attention / Oath & Law) and finger meaning.
2. Salute – Respect; same fingers touch forehead.
3. Handshake – RIGHT hand; two fingers along wrist; meaning of helping & obeying.

Leadership tip: "Describe what you're doing BEFORE you do it."

### 3:00–7:00 Teaching Round 1
Instructors teach their partners all three fundamentals. Coach them to:
- Break steps into small parts.
- Check understanding: "Show me your sign."
- Praise effort: "Nice job keeping fingers tight!"
- Correct gently: "Try holding your hand a bit higher."

Walk around giving feedback:
- "Ask your partner what the two fingers mean."
- "Great—now have them repeat it without copying you."

### 7:00–10:00 Switch Roles (If Simulated)
Have pairs switch Teacher / Learner. Repeat teaching cycle quickly focusing on clarity and confidence.

If real younger Cubs are present: Use this time for refinement and adding meaning questions:
- "When do we use the sign?"
- "Why do we salute?"
- "What do the two fingers stand for?"

### 10:00–11:30 Assessment & Leadership Reflection
Quick checks:
- Everyone shows sign together.
- Everyone shows salute.
- Pair handshake (move around to inspect form).

Ask: "What made teaching hard? What helped your partner learn?" Take 2–3 fast answers.

### 11:30–12:00 Wrap-Up
Reinforce: "Teaching is a powerful way to learn. You just demonstrated leadership by example."
Collect any worksheets if you want to review later (optional).

Say occasionally:
- "Describe first, then demonstrate."
- "Let them try it WITHOUT you doing it."
- "Ask, 'What does that mean?' to check understanding."
- "Encourage—find one thing they did well before correcting."

## Quick Reference: Fundamentals (For Fast Review)

### 1. CUB SCOUT SIGN ✌️
**When to Use:** To get attention; when reciting the Scout Oath or Law

**How to Do It:**
- Right hand up at shoulder height
- Index and middle fingers straight up (like wolf ears!)
- Thumb holds down ring finger and pinky finger
- Keep arm at shoulder level

**What It Means:** The two fingers stand for "help other people" and "obey the Scout Law"

---

### 2. CUB SCOUT SALUTE 🫡
**When to Use:** To show respect (like during Pledge of Allegiance or when flag passes)

**How to Do It:**
- Start with the Cub Scout sign (fingers like above)
- Touch the tips of your index and middle fingers to your forehead
- Keep the same finger position

**What It Means:** Saluting shows respect and honor

---

### 3. CUB SCOUT HANDSHAKE 🤝
**When to Use:** Greeting other Cub Scouts, leaders, at ceremonies

**How to Teach It:**
1. Show your **RIGHT hand** ready to shake.
2. Explain: "We use our right hand in Cub Scouts. Older Scouts use their left."
3. Demonstrate placing index and middle fingers gently along wrist.
4. Let learner try; correct finger placement if needed.

**Meaning Prompt:** "What do the two fingers remind us of?" (Answer: Helping others & obeying the Scout Law.)

---

## Teaching Tips (Leadership Emphasis)

Use the EDGE model implicitly:
- **Explain:** "This is what the sign looks like."
- **Demonstrate:** Slowly show action.
- **Guide:** Let them try; give one positive + one correction.
- **Enable:** Step back; they perform independently.

Positive Feedback Formula: "I like how you ______. Try ______ next time."

Encourage questions: "What part was confusing?" "Want to see it slower?"

---

## Common Challenges & Responses

| Challenge | What To Say |
|-----------|-------------|
| Shy learner | "You're doing great—start with just the fingers." |
| Fingers not positioned | "Curl these two down; keep these two up like ears." |
| Salute too low/high | "Forehead touch—right here. Try again." |
| Forgetting meaning | "Ask them what the two fingers mean—lead them to answer." |
| Rushing | "Slow down—teaching isn’t a race." |

## Closing Note
Focus stays on teaching accuracy and encouragement. If a scout struggles, slow down and isolate one step ("Just the fingers first"). When time is called, send group to next station promptly.
